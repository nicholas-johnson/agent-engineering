# Chores board

Build a chores board for a family: parents add chores worth points, children tick them off with a photo as proof, and at the end of the week the points turn into rewards the parents choose. It needs two kinds of user, photo uploads and a screen a seven-year-old can use, which makes it a good test of how well an app builder listens to "who is this for?".

**Build it in:** Lovable. **Time:** an evening. **Needs:** a Lovable account.

## Start in Plan mode

> Build a chores board for a family of five. Parents add chores, each worth some points, and say who can do them. Children tick a chore off by taking a photo, and a parent approves it. At the end of each week the points turn into rewards the parents choose, such as screen time or a later bedtime. The children's screen must work for a seven-year-old who can't read well.

Look at what the plan says about the seven-year-old. Did it think about pictures, colours and big buttons, or just say "simple"?

## Then, one change at a time

- "Each child picks an animal as their avatar, and sees it grow as they earn points."
- "Some chores repeat every day, some every Saturday, some once."
- "Show a parent a summary on Sunday evening: who did what, and what they've earned."
- "A child can swap a chore with a brother or sister, if both agree."

## Watch for

- **Two kinds of user.** How does it tell a parent from a child? Could a child approve their own chore?
- **The photos.** Where are they stored, and who can see them? Ask in Chat mode.
- **Tone.** Read every message a child would see. Is it kind when a chore is turned down?

## Try this

1. Put your family's real chores and rewards in the project's Instructions, and ask it to fill the board.
2. Use the preview toolbar to point at the child's screen and say what a seven-year-old would get wrong.
3. Add a "family goal": everyone's points together unlock a day out.
4. Ask it to make the whole app work offline on a tablet, then sync. What does it say?
5. Show the children's screen to an actual child. Fix the first three things they struggle with.

## Questions

For discussion. There are no right answers.

- This app stores photos of children in their home. What would you want to know about where they go before you used it for real?
- Points and rewards change how children feel about chores. Should a tool that builds the app have an opinion about that?
- Which parts of this would you trust a seven-year-old to test, and which only an adult?
