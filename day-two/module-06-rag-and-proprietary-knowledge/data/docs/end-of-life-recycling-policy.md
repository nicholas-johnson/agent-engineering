# End-of-Life Recycling Policy

## Purpose and scope

This policy provides guidance for responsibly recycling Dormouse devices and their batteries at end of life. It covers the Dormouse Thermostat, Dormouse Radiator Valve, Dormouse Room Sensor and the batteries used in these products.

Do not put Dormouse devices or batteries in household rubbish. Use an appropriate local collection or recycling service, and follow its instructions for preparing and handing in the items.

## Identify the device and its power source

| Product | Power source |
|---|---|
| Dormouse Thermostat | Mains power through its wall plate, with two AA backup batteries |
| Dormouse Radiator Valve | Two AA batteries |
| Dormouse Room Sensor | One CR2032 coin cell |

Check the product and its battery compartment before recycling. Remove batteries where they are designed to be removed, and take them to a battery collection point. Recycle the device separately through a suitable electronics collection service.

## Preparing a thermostat for end of life

Before giving away or disposing of a thermostat, remove its link to your account and erase its stored Wi-Fi details and schedules by performing a factory reset:

1. Hold the dial and the boost button down together for 10 seconds.
2. Release them when the display shows RESET.
3. Let the thermostat restart.

The factory reset removes the Wi-Fi details, schedules and link to your account. It does not replace the need to recycle the thermostat and its batteries through appropriate services.

The thermostat is wired to the boiler by a qualified installer. If it is being removed from the wall, arrange for a qualified installer to disconnect it.

## Handling and recycling batteries

Take batteries to a designated battery collection point or another service that accepts household batteries. Do not put batteries in household rubbish, and do not place loose batteries in an electronics recycling container unless that service specifically instructs you to do so.

The thermostat's two AA backup batteries are behind its front cover. To reach them, pull the bottom of the cover gently towards you. The Radiator Valve uses two AA batteries. The Room Sensor uses one CR2032 coin cell. Follow the collection service's instructions for handling and packaging each battery type.

## Recycling devices

Use a local electronics recycling or collection service that accepts the relevant device. Check the service's requirements before handing anything in, particularly if it requires batteries to be removed or devices to be separated by type.

The thermostat has a mains-powered wall plate and is wired to the boiler. Have a qualified installer disconnect it before recycling. Do not attempt to remove wiring unless you are qualified to do so.

## Data and account considerations

A factory reset removes the thermostat's Wi-Fi details, schedules and account link. Reset the thermostat before passing it to another household or sending it for recycling.

The thermostat can also be removed from the home through the Dormouse app or web dashboard. Only the owner can add or remove devices. If you need help with an account or device, contact support in the app under Help > Contact support.

## Safe handling

Handle devices and batteries carefully while preparing them for collection. Keep removed batteries away from children and pets, and do not dismantle or damage batteries or devices. Follow the instructions provided by your local collection service.

For routine care, wipe the thermostat with a soft, dry cloth. Do not use sprays or cleaning products, and do not paint over it. These cleaning instructions do not change the requirement to use an appropriate recycling service at end of life.

## Local recycling requirements

Collection options and preparation rules vary by area. Check with your local authority or recycling provider for current arrangements and accepted items. Follow its directions for separating batteries, electronics and any packaging.

If a collection service does not accept a Dormouse device or battery, use another appropriate service rather than putting the item in household rubbish.
